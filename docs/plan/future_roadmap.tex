\chapter*{Purpose}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Purpose}
\markboth{Purpose}{}

This document is deliberately unflattering. The user and technical guides
describe what Poraqu\^e does; this one records what it does \emph{not} do, how
far off it is where it falls short, and what would have to be built to close
each gap.

Every number quoted here was measured on the shipped dataset during the
26.8.19 cycle, and the measurement is named so it can be repeated. Where a
limitation is fundamental to the method rather than a defect in the code, that
is stated --- the distinction decides whether the fix is an afternoon or a
research programme.

\begin{pnote}
The single most important fact in this document: \textbf{the electrostatic
machinery is correct and the difficulty lies elsewhere}. Poraqu\^e's
$E_{\alpha Z}$, Ewald and Hartree terms reproduce VASP's \opt{PSCENC},
\opt{TEWEN} and \opt{DENC} to better than $0.01$~eV in a difference. The
accuracy problems below come from what the pseudo-valence fields
\emph{omit}, not from how they are integrated.
\end{pnote}

\chapter{Where the method stands}

\section{What is verified}

The following are checked against exact references and hold to the tolerance
quoted. They are not expected to be sources of future error.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Reference & Agreement \\
\midrule
Ewald energy & sc/bcc/fcc Madelung constants & $3\times10^{-6}$ rel. \\
Ewald forces & finite differences of the energy & $4\times10^{-9}$ eV/\AA \\
Electron--ion force & finite differences of $\int\rho\vext$ & $10^{-7}$ eV/\AA \\
$E_{\alpha Z}$ & VASP \opt{PSCENC} & $2\times10^{-4}$ eV \\
Ewald energy & VASP \opt{TEWEN} ($\Delta$) & $0.004$ eV \\
Hartree energy & VASP \opt{DENC} ($\Delta$) & $0.001$ eV \\
PBE exchange & Dirac limit on a uniform gas & machine precision \\
PW92 correlation & published values & machine precision \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{What is not accurate}

\subsection{Energy differences}

With VASP's \emph{own} $\rho$ and $\tau$ --- that is, with the operators
removed from the problem entirely --- energy differences between gold
structures of the same composition carry a mean absolute error of
$0.18$--$0.24$~eV per atom. The true differences span $0.125$~eV per atom.
\textbf{The error is larger than the signal.}

Running the full pipeline with the shipped operators, the error rises to
$0.87$--$1.93$~eV per atom.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Source & MAE on $\Delta E$ & Relative to signal \\
\midrule
Exact fields (method ceiling)      & 0.18--0.24 eV/atom & $1.5$--$2\times$ \\
Predicted fields (as shipped)      & 0.87--1.93 eV/atom & $7$--$15\times$ \\
VASP spread being resolved         & 0.125 eV/atom      & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ceiling is set by the neglected PAW one-centre and non-local
pseudopotential energies. Those are often described as a per-atom constant,
and to a first approximation they are: the offset between Poraqu\^e and VASP is
constant to about a part in $10^{4}$ across the dataset. But the constant is
$\approx 1154$~eV per atom, so a part in $10^{4}$ of it is still $0.1$~eV per
atom --- comparable to everything being predicted.

\begin{pwarn}
The total energy is a cancellation of terms of order $10^{4}$~eV down to a
result of order $10^{0}$~eV. Getting $\Delta E$ right to $10$~meV/atom
therefore demands a relative accuracy of about $10^{-6}$ in the fields. The
operators currently deliver $10^{-2}$.
\end{pwarn}

\subsection{Forces}

Forces were implemented this cycle (\pyclass{poraque.physics.forces}) and the
implementation is exact: each analytic force agrees with a finite difference of
the term it differentiates to $10^{-7}$~eV/\AA. The \emph{physics} is
incomplete.

Against VASP's \opt{TOTAL-FORCE}, using VASP's own density, the mean absolute
error is $0.83$~eV/\AA\ on forces whose mean magnitude is $1.66$~eV/\AA. The
correlation with the reference is $+0.39$ and $-0.01$ on the two structures
that carry an \file{OUTCAR}. The magnitude is right; the direction is not.

Two reasons, in order of size:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Hellmann--Feynman force is complete only for a local
      pseudopotential.} A PAW dataset contributes a projector force and a
      one-centre force, and neither is recoverable from $\rho$, $\tau$ and
      $V_{\mathrm{loc}}$ sampled on a grid. Gold's $5d$ projectors are strong.
\item \textbf{The cancellation amplifies density error.} The electron--ion and
      ion--ion terms are each $\approx 100$~eV/\AA\ and cancel to
      $\approx 0.5$~eV/\AA. A relative error $\epsilon$ in $\rho$ therefore
      arrives in the force amplified roughly $200$-fold.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Charge normalisation}

The shipped \opt{ext2chg} operator predicts densities whose electron count
drifts by up to $1.7\%$ --- nearly five electrons out of $297$. Since every
electrostatic term is at least linear in $\rho$ and the Hartree energy is
quadratic, this alone moved total energies by tens of eV, and by a different
amount for each structure, so it did not cancel in a difference.

This is now repaired at inference: \pyclass{ChargeDensity.normalized} rescales
to the count the pseudopotentials fix, which is exactly known. It halved the
end-to-end $\Delta E$ error. It is a \emph{repair}, not a cure --- a density
whose integral is wrong by $1.7\%$ has the wrong shape too, and rescaling does
not fix the shape.

\chapter{What needs to be implemented}

\section{Priority 1: the PAW one-centre and non-local terms}

This is the single change that would move Poraqu\^e from qualitative to
quantitative, and it gates both energies and forces.

\begin{description}
\item[What is missing] The one-centre (augmentation-sphere) kinetic, Hartree
  and exchange--correlation energies, and the non-local projector energy
  $\sum_{ij}\rho_{ij}D_{ij}$. Together these are $\approx 1154$~eV per atom for
  gold, of which the environment-dependent part is the few eV that decides
  every energy difference.
\item[Why it is hard] They are not functionals of the plane-wave density. They
  need the PAW occupancies $\rho_{ij}$, which live inside the augmentation
  spheres and are written to \file{CHGCAR} as the augmentation records that
  \pyclass{collect\_augmentation} currently copies verbatim without
  interpreting.
\item[The tractable route] The augmentation records \emph{are} in the data, and
  the \file{POTCAR} parser already reads the radial tables. A third learned map
  --- $\rho \mapsto \{\rho_{ij}\}$, an on-site quantity with a small output
  dimension per atom --- is a far better-posed learning problem than the field
  maps, because the target is a handful of numbers per atom rather than a
  field. Combined with an implementation of the one-centre energy expressions,
  this would close most of the gap.
\item[Effort] Substantial. Weeks, and it requires reading the PAW formalism
  carefully rather than pattern-matching VASP output.
\end{description}

\section{Priority 2: charge normalisation as a training constraint}

Normalising at inference repairs the symptom. The operator should be
\emph{unable} to produce a density with the wrong electron count in the first
place, by construction rather than by correction.

\begin{ptip}
The machinery is half-present: \pyclass{poraque.ml.physics.electron\_count\_loss}
exists and \opt{training.physics.electron\_count\_weight} is wired through the
config, but it defaults to zero and the shipped model was trained without it.
A hard normalisation \emph{head} --- rescaling the network's output to the
target count inside the forward pass, so the constraint holds exactly and the
gradient is aware of it --- is the stronger option and does not exist yet.
Compare the two against the same split before choosing.
\end{ptip}

\section{Priority 3: chemical transferability}

The dataset is 17 gold structures. Nothing in any reported number speaks to
whether the operators transfer across chemistry, and the reports say so in
their caveat box. Before Poraqu\^e can be claimed to predict densities, it needs
a dataset spanning at least a period and a few bonding types.

This also gates the validation that \file{tests/test\_energy\_differences.py}
is written for but cannot currently run: $N_2$ against isolated nitrogen, $CO$,
and the cohesive energy of diamond. The tests exist and skip; they need the
reference calculations \emph{and} operators that have seen those elements.

\section{Priority 4: spin polarisation, validated}

The \opt{ISPIN=2} code path was implemented this cycle and is exercised only by
synthetic fixtures:

\begin{itemize}
\item \pyclass{SpinDensity} reads and writes VASP's two-block \file{CHGCAR}
      and round-trips to full precision;
\item \pyclass{FieldPairDataset} detects two channels from the file rather than
      from a flag, and refuses a flag that contradicts the data;
\item the two channels are normalised separately
      (\pyclass{transforms.Channelwise}), because $\rho$ integrates to hundreds
      of electrons while $m$ integrates to a few $\mu_B$ and one shared scale
      normalises neither;
\item the FNO takes and returns two channels, and the counts survive a
      checkpoint round trip.
\end{itemize}

What is missing is any spin-polarised reference data at all. Until an
\opt{ISPIN=2} dataset exists, none of this is known to be \emph{correct},
only self-consistent.

\begin{pnote}
One physics gap is already known within the implemented path: the
exchange--correlation energy in \pyclass{poraque.physics.energy} is
spin-unpolarised. Feeding it a spin-polarised system through
\pyclass{SpinDensity.as\_charge\_density} silently evaluates the LDA/PBE
functional on the total density, which is wrong for a magnetic system.
Implementing the spin-scaling relation for exchange and the PW92 polarised
branch for correlation is well-defined, textbook work and should accompany the
first real \opt{ISPIN=2} dataset.
\end{pnote}

\section{Priority 5: the stress tensor}

\pyclass{Poraque.get\_stress} still raises. The stress needs the energy's
response to a strain, which deforms the cell \emph{and} the grid the fields
live on --- so it is not simply another Hellmann--Feynman term. Given that
forces are not yet accurate enough for relaxation, the stress is correctly
sequenced after Priority 1.

\chapter{Known limitations}

\section{Fundamental to the method}

\begin{description}
\item[Absolute energies are not DFT energies.] The fields are pseudo-valence
  quantities and VASP's atomic reference \opt{EATOM} is absent. The offset is
  $\approx 1154$~eV per atom for gold. This will never be fixed and does not
  need to be --- only differences are claimed.
\item[The energy is a difference of large numbers.] Discussed above. It sets a
  ceiling on what any field accuracy can buy and should inform how much effort
  goes into the operators versus into the missing terms.
\item[An FNO is resolution-flexible, not resolution-indifferent.] Evaluating
  far from the training grid density extrapolates in a way no metric in the
  package detects. The inference script warns; nothing enforces.
\item[Symbolic distillation searches semi-local functionals only.] The features
  are evaluated at a point, so whatever the operator learned that is non-local
  cannot appear in the distilled expression and shows up as residual. The
  reports state this beside every fit.
\end{description}

\section{Implementation limitations}

\begin{description}
\item[No forces good enough for dynamics.] Geometry optimisation and molecular
  dynamics remain out of reach. \pyclass{compute\_forces} exists and is
  documented as indicative.
\item[Gaussian pseudo-ion fallback is out of distribution.] Without a
  \file{POTCAR} the external potential falls back to a Gaussian model that
  differs from the tabulated one by a relative $L^2$ of $\approx0.13$, far
  outside anything the operators saw. The calculator warns once and proceeds;
  forces refuse outright, since there is no form factor to differentiate.
\item[Spin-polarised exchange--correlation is not implemented.] See the note
  above.
\item[$\tau$ is single-channel under \opt{ISPIN=2}.] VASP writes one
  \file{TAUCAR} block regardless, so \opt{chg2tau} is two channels in and one
  out. Whether the spin-resolved kinetic energy density is needed for a
  spin-polarised kinetic functional is an open question, not a settled one.
\end{description}

\chapter{Areas needing more rigorous testing}

The suite is 602 tests and covers the numerics well. The gaps below are not
about coverage percentages --- they are the specific claims that are currently
\emph{unverified}, ordered by how badly a silent failure would hurt.

\section{Untested because the data does not exist}

\begin{description}
\item[Molecular and cohesive energetics.]
  \file{tests/test\_energy\_differences.py} contains the $N_2$, $CO$ and
  diamond cases the project needs, parameterised against a documented layout
  under \opt{PORAQUE\_REFERENCE\_DATA}. They skip. These are differences
  between systems of \emph{different} composition, where the per-atom offset
  does not cancel --- the hardest thing that will ever be asked of the
  calculator, and currently the least tested.
\item[Forces on a perturbed molecule.] \file{tests/test\_forces.py} carries
  stretched-$N_2$ and stretched-$CO$ cases which skip for the same reason. A
  stretched diatomic is the cleanest possible force test: one bond, one
  non-zero component per atom, equal and opposite.
\item[Anything spin-polarised.] All 25 spin tests use synthetic fixtures.
\end{description}

\section{Tested too narrowly}

\begin{description}
\item[Forces against VASP.] Two structures, both gold, both from the same
  generation run --- they are the only ones in the dataset carrying an
  \file{OUTCAR}. The force comparison is written as a characterisation with a
  regression bound, not as a physics claim, precisely because two structures of
  one element cannot support one.
\item[Energy differences.] Seventeen structures, one element, two distinct
  atom counts. The per-atom-constant assumption underlying every difference is
  verified only within gold.
\item[Resolution convergence.] Energies were compared at $32^3$ and at the
  native $\approx 128^3$ and agree to $0.2$~eV in a difference, which is
  reassuring but is two points on one system. There is no systematic
  convergence study of any reported quantity against grid density.
\end{description}

\section{Not exercised in the ordinary test run}

\begin{description}
\item[Symbolic distillation end to end.] PySR is an optional dependency
  carrying a Julia toolchain, so the tests cover the feature construction, the
  asymptotic-limit checker and the report rendering, but never run a search.
  The load-order workaround for the \opt{juliacall}/\opt{torch} segfault is
  therefore untested by CI --- and it is the kind of failure that appears only
  after an hour of training.
\item[Fine-tuning.] Configuration validation is tested; the fine-tuning path
  itself is not run against a real base checkpoint.
\item[Committee disagreement.] The ranking machinery is tested on synthetic
  ensembles. Its correlation with true error --- the only property that makes
  it useful for active learning --- is measurable with \opt{--against} but is
  not part of any test.
\end{description}

\section{Suggested order of work}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Generate reference calculations for $N_2$, isolated N, $CO$, isolated C
  and O, and diamond. This unblocks eight skipped tests and is a day of
  compute.
\item Generate one \opt{ISPIN=2} dataset --- bcc iron is the obvious choice ---
  and validate the spin path against it.
\item Train with \opt{electron\_count\_weight} enabled and measure whether it
  beats inference-time normalisation on the same split.
\item Begin the PAW one-centre work, which everything quantitative depends on.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{pnote}
Items 1--3 are each about a day and each convert an unverified claim into a
tested one. Item 4 is the research programme. The ordering is deliberate: it
would be a mistake to start item 4 while the cheap verification is still
outstanding, because item 4's success can only be measured against the tests
items 1--2 create.
\end{pnote}
